Welcome to senior year

Your student is about to apply to college, and they will not do it alone. Every SLJ senior has a college advisor, a weekly Senior Seminar class devoted to the application process, and a College & Career team that stays with them from the first application to the day they start college, and beyond.

This guide covers the whole year: what happens when, what we will need from your family, how to pay for college, and the special programs and enrichment opportunities available to your student. Keep it somewhere you can find it. The dates in here are the dates your student is being held to at school.

The single most useful thing a family can do happens in September: if you would like SLJ's support with your financial aid applications (including FAFSA, TAP, CSS Profile, and H/EOP applications), gather your 2025 financial documents and share them with us by September 25. This includes tax returns, but also benefit statements for those who do not file taxes. Sharing them is always your choice; it is how we help. See "What we'll ask from you."

The year at a glance

Senior year runs in four movements: prepare (September), apply (October–December), decide (March–May), and launch (June). Here is the full timeline.

Sept
Sept 10 · Senior Seminar orientation Sept 15 · CUNY application opens Sept 25 · Financial documents (for aid support)

Seminar begins and students choose their timeline (see the accelerated timeline below). A College & Career contract comes home for a parent signature. Students starting the CUNY application will need their Social Security number, if they have one.

Parents: sign and return the C&C contract. If you would like our help with financial aid applications, share your 2025 financial documents by Sept 25 (list below). If your student is considering an SAT sitting this fall, registration for the October test also closes Sept 15.
Oct
Oct 1 · QuestBridge application due Oct 30 · CUNY application submitted

Application season begins in earnest. Every senior submits their CUNY application (six college choices plus intended majors) by October 30. Students applying to the QuestBridge program (see Programs) have their big deadline October 1. In seminar, students create their FSA ID, the login used for federal financial aid; one parent or guardian needs one too.

Parents: when your student asks you to create an FSA ID, please do it that week. The name you register must exactly match the name on your Social Security card. And a rule that holds all year: students submit applications WITH a college advisor. Please encourage your student never to hit submit before an advisor has fully reviewed the application.
Nov
Nov 13 · Final college list Nov 26 · FAFSA submitted

Each student finalizes the list of colleges they will apply to, built with their advisor around academic, social, and financial fit. We encourage students to apply to colleges that meet 100% of demonstrated financial need, the ones that give the strongest aid packages; our list is here. The FAFSA (the federal financial aid application) opens October 1 and every family should plan to have it submitted by November 26 if possible. It takes about an hour when the tax documents are on hand.

Our key suggestion for families: run a Net Price Calculator for every college on your student's final list. It is a free tool that estimates what each college could actually cost your family per year, after aid, and the answers vary enormously. Start at bigfuture.collegeboard.org. Families on the accelerated timeline should do this in mid-October, when their list is finalized.
Parents: the FAFSA is completed entirely online at studentaid.gov, and it needs a parent's information and digital signature. Your student will invite you into the form as a contributor; please complete your section promptly. Families are welcome to make appointments to complete this with a college advisor, in person or virtually.
Dec
Dec 2 · College Crunch essay night Dec 4 · Final essay Dec 7–11 · All applications submitted Dec 14–18 · CSS Profile

The home stretch. College Crunch is an after-school essay review event with volunteers, alumni, and staff. All SUNY, private, and out-of-state applications are submitted the week of December 7, every one of them with a college advisor: students should not hit submit before an advisor has fully reviewed the application. Families applying to private colleges complete the CSS Profile, an additional aid form some private schools use, the following week.

Parents: December is stressful. The best help is protected time and space at home to write, and a few evenings free of other obligations.
Jan–Feb

Applications are in; paperwork continues. Colleges may ask for mid-year grades (we send them) or for verification of financial aid information (we will help, but some requests come straight to you). New York State aid applications (TAP, and the NYS Dream Act for undocumented students) are completed in this window.

Parents: if a college or a government aid office mails or emails you a request for documents, do not set it aside. Bring it to us if anything is confusing, and be cautious: verify any unexpected request through the college's official phone number, not a link in the message.
Mar–Apr

Decisions arrive, mostly mid-to-late March. Then the real work: comparing financial aid offers side by side to find the true cost of each option. Advisors sit with every student (families welcome) to compare packages.

Parents: please don't let your student commit anywhere before the aid offers have been compared. The sticker price is almost never the real price, and offers can differ by thousands of dollars.
May
May 1 · National Decision Day

Your student commits to a college and pays the enrollment deposit. Our May 1 checklist also asks every student to record their yearly cost, complete two short school surveys, and, if the chosen college costs more than $5,000 a year out of pocket, hold a CUNY spot as a backup while summer finances settle.

Parents: the deposit (typically $0–$500; CUNY's is $0) is due at commitment. Talk to us early if the deposit itself is a hardship; there are often options.
June
Late June · Final transcripts & records

Graduation! We send final transcripts to each student's college, students submit immunization records for college health forms, and we collect a personal email and cell number so we can keep supporting your graduate. Many opportunity programs run a mandatory summer session before freshman year, so watch for those dates.

The accelerated timeline

Students aiming for early deadlines at selective colleges (Early Decision and Early Action, typically due November 1) can opt into an accelerated timeline on September 10. Their internal dates move up: final list October 16, final essay October 30, all applications in by November 15. Students with a GPA of 85+ are encouraged to consider it. Talk it over as a family before orientation.

What we'll ask from you

A handful of documents and accounts make the year run smoothly: financial aid, opportunity programs, and the applications themselves. Here is the complete list.

By September 25: financial documents, if you would like our help with aid

You are never required to share financial documents with SLJ (colleges themselves may require them for aid). But if you would like help with your financial aid applications, including FAFSA, TAP, CSS Profile, and H/EOP applications, please share your 2025 financial documents with us by September 25:

  • Family financial form (the survey we send home; online or paper)
  • 2025 federal tax return (Form 1040, full packet incl. NY State IT-201) or a benefit statement if you do not file taxes
  • W-2s and/or 1099s from 2025

Why we ask: these documents let us screen your student for opportunity programs like HEOP that can be worth tens of thousands of dollars, prepare an accurate FAFSA, and complete the CSS Profile. They are stored securely, seen only by the college office, and used for nothing else.

Also this fall

  • Social Security card (a copy, for students who have one): the number is needed to start the CUNY application, and the exact name on the card is needed for financial aid logins
  • Signed College & Career contract: comes home at September orientation
  • Parent FSA ID: a login you create at studentaid.gov when your student asks (around October); name must match your Social Security card exactly

Through the year

  • Your section of the FAFSA: by November 26
  • Signatures when asked: some programs (for example QuestBridge) require a parent signature on program forms
  • Verification documents if requested: colleges sometimes ask families to confirm aid information (January–February)
  • The enrollment deposit: at commitment, by May 1
A note on privacy. Sharing financial documents is always your choice. What you do share is handled only by the College & Career office, used only for aid and program eligibility, and never shared with other students or families.

Paying for college

This is the part of the process families worry about most, and the part where SLJ students have the most going for them. The rule of thumb: never rule out a college by its sticker price. Aid can change the picture completely, and some of the most generous colleges in the country are also the most selective.

Opportunity programs: money plus support

New York runs remarkable programs that combine financial aid with tutoring, counseling, and a supportive community on campus. Eligibility is based on family income and academic profile; if you share your financial documents by September 25, we can screen your student for all of these:

  • HEOP: at New York private colleges (NYU, Fordham, Syracuse, and many more)
  • EOP: at SUNY campuses
  • SEEK / College Discovery: at CUNY colleges

These programs can turn a private college that looks unaffordable into the cheapest option on the list. Most include a mandatory summer program before freshman year; treat that as part of the deal, and a good one.

Worth knowing

  • When the final college list comes together in the fall, run a Net Price Calculator for every college on it to estimate what each would cost your family per year. See the highlighted note in the timeline.
  • A number of colleges, including several our students apply to, pledge to meet 100% of a family's demonstrated financial need. We keep a list and build it into college lists for strong candidates.
  • The Excelsior Scholarship can make CUNY/SUNY tuition free for many NY families; the application for your student's freshman year happens after commitment, and we flag it to graduates.
  • In spring we compare every offer's real yearly cost with the student. Families are warmly invited to those meetings.

Special programs & scholarships

Beyond the regular application, a few competitive programs offer extraordinary outcomes. Students opt into these application processes, and every family should know they exist.

QuestBridge National College Match Application due Oct 1

A national program matching high-achieving students from lower-income families to full four-year scholarships at 55 of the most selective colleges (Stanford, MIT, Columbia, Amherst, and more). SLJ students have been selected before. The application needs essays, two teacher recommendations, and your 2025 tax return, another reason gathering financial documents by September 25 pays off. Parents of participating students will also sign a Match Agreement form in mid-October.

Posse Foundation Nomination-based, fall rounds

Full-tuition leadership scholarships that send teams ("posses") of NYC students to partner colleges together. Nominated students attend selection rounds through the fall; finalists hear in December.

Macaulay Honors College With the CUNY application

CUNY's honors college: free tuition, a laptop, and an opportunities fund, at eight CUNY campuses. Strong students apply through the CUNY application with an earlier deadline; advisors flag candidates.

College fly-in & diversity visit programs Applications July–early Sept

Dozens of selective colleges fly students in for free overnight campus visits each fall. A great trial run and a real signal of interest. Applications are short (usually one essay); the window is early fall, so seniors should see their advisor in September.

New scholarships cross our desk every week during the year. When one fits your student, their advisor will bring it to them directly.

Career pathways: the "Career" in College & Career

College is the most common next step for SLJ graduates, but it is not the only one, and it is not the only thing we advise on. Every senior leaves with a concrete plan for the year after graduation, built with an advisor, whatever shape that plan takes.

Career preparation every student gets

Career exploration is woven into all four years at SLJ: students research careers and majors on Overgrad, the college and career platform they use in seminar; hear from professionals through Career Day (10th grade) and the Lunch With Lawyers series; and get real practice through mock trial, moot court, and attorney mentors. Financial literacy is built into Senior Seminar for every senior: budgeting, credit and debt, understanding loans, and reading a paycheck, useful no matter where your student is headed.

FutureReadyNYC at SLJ

SLJ is part of FutureReadyNYC, NYC Public Schools' career-pathways initiative, with a pathway focused on careers in education. Students in the FutureReadyNYC cohort earn early college credit through dual enrollment, work toward an industry credential, and complete paid, career-aligned work experience, including internships. Families of cohort students hear the details from us directly; if your student is interested in joining, ask their advisor.

Paid work experience

We encourage every student to get paid work experience before graduation. The Summer Youth Employment Program (SYEP), New York City's paid summer jobs program, is the most common route; applications open in the winter and we help students apply. Paid internships also run through the school's enrichment programs.

Parents: paid placements require working papers (for students under 18) and proof of work eligibility (the I-9: for example a Social Security card plus school or government ID). Having these documents ready is the single best way to help your student say yes quickly when a paid opportunity appears.

If college is not the plan, or not the plan yet

Some students are weighing full-time work, a trade or apprenticeship program, city workforce programs, military service, or a year to work and save before enrolling. That is a real plan, and it deserves the same advising as any college list. What we do together: map the options, connect the student to programs, and keep doors open. Every senior still completes the CUNY application, because it costs nothing extra, keeps an affordable college option live, and can be deferred or used later. Our May 1 checklist covers every graduate, not just the college-bound: everyone leaves with a committed next step, and we stay in touch after graduation to help when plans evolve.

Enrichment beyond the classroom

College readiness is not only paperwork. Through the Adams Street Foundation, SLJ funds enrichment that most schools cannot, and it is open to every student regardless of GPA.

  • Study abroad and travel programs
  • Paid internships during the school year and summer
  • Residential pre-college programs on university campuses
  • Law & justice experiences: mock trial and moot court, courthouse trips, attorney mentors who coach students on arguments and college essays, and the monthly Lunch With Lawyers series
  • Campus visits for every grade, plus an overnight multi-college trip
  • After-school clubs, snowboarding trips, and the Young Men's and Young Women's Initiatives

Opportunities are announced through advisories, email, and the Enrichment office all year. Encourage your student to say yes to things. As we like to say at SLJ: school is not a rehearsal for life, it is life.

What to do at home, month by month

  • September: the signed contract, a conversation about the accelerated timeline, and your financial documents if you would like our help with aid applications.
  • October: create your FSA ID the week your student asks. Ask them what their six CUNY choices are.
  • November: sit down together for the FAFSA. Ask to see the final college list, and run a Net Price Calculator for every college on it.
  • December: quiet writing time at home. Celebrate when the applications go in; it is a big deal.
  • January–February: open every letter and email from colleges and aid offices; forward anything confusing to us.
  • March–April: come to the aid-comparison meeting. Keep an open mind; the best offer is often a surprise.
  • May: commit by May 1, pay the deposit, and let us know how it feels. We want to celebrate with you.
  • June: watch for summer program dates from the college, and make sure we have a personal email and cell for your graduate.

Glossary: every acronym in one place

The college process has a language of its own. Dotted-underlined terms throughout this guide jump here; hover over them on a computer for a quick definition.

C&C
College & Career: SLJ's college and career office, the team behind this guide and your student's advising.
Common App
One online application accepted by most private colleges (commonapp.org).
CSS Profile
A second financial aid form used by many private colleges to award their own institutional aid. Fee waivers available.
CUNY
The City University of New York: NYC's 25 public colleges. One application covers six choices.
NYS Dream Act
New York State financial aid for undocumented students, filed in place of the FAFSA.
ED / EA / RD
Early Decision (binding, ~Nov 1), Early Action (early but not binding), Regular Decision (January). The application rounds at private colleges.
EOP
Educational Opportunity Program: aid plus on-campus support at SUNY campuses, based on family income and academics.
Excelsior Scholarship
NY State program that can make CUNY/SUNY tuition free for many families. Applied for after commitment.
FAFSA
Free Application for Federal Student Aid: the online form (studentaid.gov) behind nearly all grants, loans, and work-study.
FutureReadyNYC
NYC Public Schools' career-pathways program: college credit, an industry credential, and paid work experience for cohort students.
FSA ID
The username and password for federal aid at studentaid.gov. The student and one parent each need their own; names must match Social Security cards exactly.
HEOP
Higher Education Opportunity Program: aid plus support at New York private colleges, based on family income and academics.
I-9
The federal work-eligibility form for any paid job; needs documents like a Social Security card plus an ID.
Macaulay
Macaulay Honors College: CUNY's honors program with free tuition, applied to through the CUNY application.
Net Price Calculator
A tool on every college's website (and collected at bigfuture.collegeboard.org) that estimates what that college would actually cost your family per year, after aid.
Overgrad
The online college and career platform students use in seminar to research colleges and careers, build their list, and complete school surveys.
Pell Grant
The main federal grant (money that is never paid back), awarded through the FAFSA.
Posse
The Posse Foundation: full-tuition leadership scholarships sending teams of NYC students to partner colleges together.
QuestBridge
National College Match: full four-year scholarships at 55 of the most selective colleges, for high-achieving students from lower-income families.
SEEK / College Discovery
CUNY's opportunity programs: aid plus support at four-year (SEEK) and community (College Discovery) colleges.
SUNY
The State University of New York: 64 public campuses across the state.
SYEP
Summer Youth Employment Program: NYC's paid summer jobs program for young people. Applications open in winter.
TAP
Tuition Assistance Program: New York State aid for study at CUNY, SUNY, and NY private colleges. Invitation arrives after the FAFSA.
Working papers
The NYC employment certificate students under 18 need for a paid job; issued through school.
