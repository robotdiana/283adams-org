Supporting Students. Expanding Possibilities.

Founded in 2008, the Adams Street Foundation funds and implements high-quality, individualized programming and counseling for low-income, underserved high school students, to prepare them for success in college and career.

Who we are

SLJ’s nonprofit affiliate

As the non-profit affiliate of the Urban Assembly School for Law & Justice, ASF serves its students — the majority of whom live near or below the poverty line and will be the first in their family to go to college — by integrating pre-college enrichment, and college and career preparation into the school’s curriculum.

Every SLJ student receives personalized support throughout the college process: one-on-one college advising, essay and application support, financial aid and scholarship guidance, and opportunities to explore professional and career pathways. ASF-funded enrichment — study abroad, paid internships, residential pre-college programs, courthouse trips, and after-school clubs — is open to every student, regardless of GPA.

Impact

What your support makes possible

SLJ students — many entering high school below grade level, more than 75% the first in their families to attend college — have earned admission to institutions including Williams, Cornell, Georgetown, Howard, the University of Pennsylvania, the University of Chicago, and Yale.

Getting in
100%of seniors apply to college and are accepted
Nationally, 52% of graduates from low-income high schools enroll in college at all (National Student Clearinghouse, 2024)
Staying in college
87%of graduates still enrolled 18 months later
At comparable high-poverty high schools nationally, about 38 of every 100 graduates are still enrolled a year later (National Student Clearinghouse)
Time and attention
≈65:1students per college advisor at SLJ
372:1 is the national counselor ratio, and only a third of US public high schools have any counselor dedicated to college at all (ASCA; NACAC)

Our work

The part nobody sees

What ends a student’s path to college is almost never a rejection letter. It is a password that won’t reset, a tax transcript a family cannot obtain, a deposit deadline that passes quietly in July. Our advisors follow through on every single student, one on one, from junior year through four years past graduation.
